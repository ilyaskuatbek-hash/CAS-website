Common misunderstandings

Many students underestimate CAS.

A lot of students think CAS is just an extra task to complete before graduation. In reality, it is a chance to grow, reflect, and make a difference in a meaningful way.

Why students struggle

CAS often feels unclear at first.

❓

“What am I supposed to do?”

Many students do not know how to begin because CAS is broad and personal. It is not limited to one type of activity or one right answer.

⏳

“It seems too time-consuming.”

Students often underestimate the value of reflection and long-term commitment. They see CAS as a burden instead of an opportunity for growth.

💭

“I only need to check a box.”

Some students focus only on meeting the requirement and miss the real purpose: learning, development, and service to others.

What CAS really is

CAS becomes meaningful when students stop thinking of it as a chore.

The goal is not to complete tasks just for the sake of completion. The goal is to learn something about yourself, challenge yourself, and contribute positively to the world around you.

When students understand that CAS is personal and purposeful, it becomes much more than an assignment. It becomes a chance to build character and impact others in a genuine way.

🧠

Unclear starts

Students often need guidance before they can see how CAS fits their interests.

🌟

Real growth

When approached correctly, CAS builds confidence, empathy, and leadership.

The solution

Break CAS down into clear, practical actions.

Start with self-reflection

Ask yourself what you enjoy, what you are good at, and what kind of impact you want to have.

Look for small opportunities

CAS does not need to be huge. It can begin with a local project, a club, or a personal challenge.

Choose meaningful work

Pick activities that connect to your interests and values, not just what looks impressive.

Learn from examples

The best way to understand CAS is to see how each strand works in real life.
