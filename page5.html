Frequently asked questions

Clear answers to get you started.

What does CAS stand for?

CAS means Creativity, Activity, and Service—the three strands of this part of the IB Diploma Programme core.

Is CAS only for students who are already talented?

No. CAS is an opportunity to develop interests and skills, take on challenges, and learn through experience. You can start as a beginner.

What activities can count as CAS?

Possibilities include creative work, physical activities, and experiences that contribute to others. Your school CAS coordinator can help decide whether a specific idea is suitable.

Do I have to do all three strands?

CAS includes Creativity, Activity, and Service. Students should plan a balanced range of experiences across the strands; ask your coordinator how your school's programme applies this.

What is a CAS project?

It is a collaborative experience where students work together over an extended period. The project should involve planning, problem-solving, and reflection; check the official IB guidance and your school's instructions for details.

Why do I need to reflect?

Reflection helps you notice what you learned, how you responded to challenges, and how an experience affected you or others. It is about thoughtful learning, not writing for its own sake.

CAS is not assessed with a formal exam, but students are expected to meet their programme's CAS requirements. Requirements and documentation are managed by the school, so confirm the details with your CAS coordinator.
